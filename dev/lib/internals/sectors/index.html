Polynomial seed and sector maps

Polynomial multiplication supplies a rank decomposition for the initial tensor. The separation, midpoint, and tripling operations turn that seed into the sector restrictions used by the finite construction.

Polynomial seed

NineFourths.LEG_PERMUTATIONS — Constant
LEG_PERMUTATIONS

The six ordered factor orientations in Q(a,b). Each tuple maps a leg of the balanced seed to its original leg in C(a,b). The first orientation varies fastest in the combined tensor coordinates.

source
NineFourths.PolynomialDecomposition — Type
PolynomialDecomposition(dimensions, order)

Fourier rank decomposition of the polynomial multiplication tensor C(a,b). Store the three leg lengths and the root order as BigInt values. Each evaluation point gives one rank-one term, whose coefficients are generated individually by rank_coefficient. Use polynomial_decomposition to choose the dimensions and order.

source
NineFourths.SeedDecomposition — Type
SeedDecomposition(polynomial, copies, dimensions, terms)

Rank decomposition of Q(a,b) raised to copies. Store its base PolynomialDecomposition, its BigInt copy count, three leg lengths, and rank-term count. Coefficients are generated by mixed-radix indexing into the six orientations of each copy. Use seed_decomposition to compute the metadata from a, b, and the copy count.

NineFourths.baseline_source_coordinate — Method
baseline_source_coordinate(dimensions, scalar_leg, leg, matrix_size, digit_base, coordinate)::BigInt

Map a one-based matrix coordinate of the baseline restriction to its source coordinate in leg leg of Q(a,b). Here dimensions contains the three leg lengths of C(a,b), scalar_leg is the shorter input leg, digit_base is the longer input length, and matrix_size is digit_base^2.

In each orientation, the shorter input is fixed to its constant term. The remaining dot product shares one digit of the row, inner, or column index depending on the position of that scalar leg. Matrix coordinates use Julia column-major order.

check_rank_coordinate(decomposition, term, leg, coordinate)

Check integer rank-term, leg, and coordinate indices, all one-based. The decomposition must support rank_count and a three-leg size tuple. Throw BoundsError on the first invalid index. Return nothing on success.

NineFourths.polynomial_decomposition — Method
polynomial_decomposition(a, b; admissible_order=nothing)

Return a PolynomialDecomposition by evaluating and interpolating at roots of unity, as in Theorem 3.1. The input lengths a and b are positive integers. With a Boolean admissible_order(order) predicate, try the BigInt orders a + b - 1 and a + b in that order. Both exceed the product degree. For field arithmetic the predicate should test that the order is invertible. With nothing, use a + b - 1.

NineFourths.rank_coefficient — Function
rank_coefficient(decomposition, term::Integer, leg::Integer, coordinate::Integer)

Return one entry of a rank-one factor as a Rational{BigInt} or PhaseSum. All indices are one-based and are checked against the decomposition's dimensions and rank_count.

For PolynomialDecomposition, the input legs use Fourier evaluation and the output leg uses the inverse transform. For SeedDecomposition, decode the indices across copies and orientations in first-factor-fastest order and multiply the corresponding Fourier coefficients.

Return a SeedDecomposition of Q(a,b)⊗m for positive integer lengths a and b and a nonnegative copy count m. The first tensor factor varies fastest. At m = 0, the result has one rank term and one coordinate on each leg. Use admissible_order as in polynomial_decomposition.

NineFourths.midpoint_column — Method
midpoint_column(emit, a, b, s, leg, copy, index, t)::Nothing

Substitute a source variable of C(a,b) ⊗ B_X(2) as in Lemma 5.6.

Call emit(coordinate, coefficient) for each term in its image. The lengths a, b and shift s are integers with a ≥ 1 and 1 ≤ s < b. leg, copy and index are one-based integers. t is a nonzero exact rational or an invertible PhaseSum monomial, as accepted by evaluation_parameter.

The callback receives a Rational{BigInt} or PhaseSum coefficient. The coordinate is an integer on leg 1 and a pair of integers (sector, index) on legs 2 and 3. Sector 1 is the b-s kernel and sector 2 is the b+s complement.

NineFourths.separation_column — Function
separation_column(emit, sector_type, leg, ρ, coordinates, t,
                  rootpower=nothing; admissible_order=nothing)::Nothing

Substitute a source variable of I_(2M) ⊗ (∑ᵢ Tᵢ)^⊗N using Lemmas 5.1 and 5.5.

sector_type is a SectorType and M is its BigInt multiplicity. leg is an integer in 1:3, and ρ is an integer copy index in 0:2M-1. admissible_order is nothing for L = 2M or an integer equal to 2M or 2M-1 as in §7. The first L copies are active. t is a nonzero exact rational or an invertible PhaseSum monomial, as accepted by evaluation_parameter.

The finite iterable coordinates supplies one integer index per factor on leg 1 or one pair of integers (sector, index) per factor on legs 2 and 3. Words outside the fixed type are dropped. emit(coordinate, coefficient) receives one term at a time. Each image coordinate is (type_rank, dot_index, canonical_indices) with two one-based integers and a tuple of integer indices. The first leg uses dot_index == 1.

rootpower is nothing or a callable mapping an integer exponent e to the eth power of a primitive Lth root of unity. Its result must support multiplication by the exact evaluation coefficient. With the default root, the emitted coefficient is a Rational{BigInt} or PhaseSum.

NineFourths.tripling_column — Method
tripling_column(emit, a, b, leg, index, t)::Nothing

Substitute a source variable of C(a,3b+a-1) as in Lemma 5.2.

Call emit(coordinate, coefficient) for each term in its image. The lengths a and b are positive integers, and leg and index are one-based integers. t is a nonzero exact rational or an invertible PhaseSum monomial, as accepted by evaluation_parameter.

The callback receives a Rational{BigInt} or PhaseSum coefficient. The coordinate is an integer on leg 1 and a pair of integers (sector, index) on legs 2 and 3. The middle sector still has its first-input indices reversed and its second and third legs exchanged.

Midpoint and tripling

NineFourths._SECTOR_DOT_LEGS — Constant
_SECTOR_DOT_LEGS

Map each balanced leg's two matrix axes to the legs shared by their dot factors.

B_X uses the column index, B_Y the row index, and B_Z the contraction index. The axes on legs 1, 2 and 3 are ordered as (row, contraction), (contraction, column) and (row, column), respectively. Each axis combines the digits from two orientations.

NineFourths.transition_restriction — Method
transition_restriction(
    transition::SectorTransition, stage::Integer, t; admissible_order=nothing
)::TensorRestriction

Build the exact restriction at parameter t for one stage of Claim 6.6.

Stage 1 maps source factors ordered as the unit tensor, balanced seed copies, and the matrix tensor when needed for a midpoint. Its intermediate target contains the unit tensor and six oriented sector tensors per copy. Stage 2 maps these intermediate coordinates to the returned unit tensor, child seed copies, and matrix tensor. Shorter child copies come first for a midpoint. Each leg map has one-based target rows and source columns. The first factor varies fastest.

t is a nonzero exact rational or an invertible PhaseSum monomial, as accepted by evaluation_parameter. For M = transition.sector_type.multiplicity, admissible_order is nothing to use 2M or an integer equal to 2M or 2M-1. Choose an order invertible in the target field. The second stage keeps the physical unit coordinates in radix 2M. For the smaller order, the extra digit is discarded in each orientation as in §7.
