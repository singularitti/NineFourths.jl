Design Principles

  • Follow the construction
  • Preserve the coordinate conventions
  • Keep arithmetic in the input field
  • Verify identities and enforce execution limits

NineFourths turns the proof of a matrix multiplication exponent bound into a finite bilinear scheme. Its main responsibility is to preserve the tensor identities from the derivation through coefficient access and execution.

Follow the construction

src/construction/parameters.jl and derivation.jl choose the exact parameters and record the baseline, restriction, symmetry, midpoint, and tripling steps. polynomial.jl, separation.jl, and sectors.jl implement the polynomial seed and the substitutions for individual steps. compile.jl composes those steps, interpolates the degenerations, and prepares and reuses the auxiliary tensors. Use the lemma, claim, or equation number to explain the origin of a formula. Shpilka's exposition, §§3–7.

The constructed scheme stores operations and exact parameters. Indexing a Shpilka scheme gives a deferred coefficient. Keep powers and dimensions symbolic when expanding them would dominate the work.

Preserve the coordinate conventions

In src/maps.jl, a leg map has source columns and target rows. The column for one source variable gives its substitution into the target variables. A sparse row callback may emit several contributions to the same source column. Their sum must equal the corresponding indexed coefficient.

Tensor products pack the first factor fastest. Matrix coordinates use Julia's one-based column-major order. When changing a permutation, restriction, or packing rule, check that each map's target coordinates match the next map's source coordinates.

Keep arithmetic in the input field

The executor reads the operands' scalar field and returns a dense Julia matrix whose elements use that field. Julia integer and rational inputs use unbounded exact arithmetic. Custom scalars can obtain their identities from a field parent and need only the scalar operations used by the calculation. The executor also accepts matrix containers with compatible dimensions, axes, and scalar indexing.

Fourier separation and interpolation use orders that are invertible in the chosen field. Temporary cyclotomic expressions carry base-field weights. Project to the base field after assembling the product. A change to coefficient embedding or projection should preserve both the multiplication identity and the element type and parent of the result.

Verify identities and enforce execution limits

Verification of a Shpilka scheme checks the symbolic construction and its exponent bound. Verification of explicit coefficient tables checks their action on pairs of matrix units. Tests also contract a compiled midpoint restriction and exercise the compiled polynomial baseline over finite fields. These checks cover tensor identities that an exponent calculation alone cannot establish.

The execution planner checks index bounds, coefficient visits, and dense workspace before applying a scheme layer. Execution pads the row, inner, and column dimensions independently. A classical product is available when the caller explicitly chooses fallback=:classical.
